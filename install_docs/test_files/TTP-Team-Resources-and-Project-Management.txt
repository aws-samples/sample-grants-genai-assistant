Team Qualifications, Resource Requirements, and Project Management: Nanoscale Thermal Transport Research Program

1. PRINCIPAL INVESTIGATOR EXPERTISE

The PI has designed, built, and validated a time-domain thermoreflectance (TDTR) system incorporating a frequency-doubled pump beam, an expanded probe beam in coaxial geometry, and a single-objective-lens optical imaging system for rapid alignment and focusing. This system has been used to measure thermal transport in bulk liquids, in highly ordered pyrolytic graphite (extracting both cross-plane and in-plane thermal conductivity), and in semiconductor superlattices. The PI has developed a linear-systems analytical framework for interpreting TDTR measurements that unifies the treatment of pulse accumulation and radial (including anisotropic) heat conduction, extending the isotropic multilayer solution to anisotropic materials at no additional computational cost. The PI has also measured thermal conductance at hydrophilic and hydrophobic solid-liquid interfaces, demonstrating that interface chemistry measurably and repeatably affects thermal boundary conductance.

2. RESEARCH METHODOLOGY SUMMARY (for cross-reference with Implementation Plan)

All measurements use the pump-probe TDTR approach: a metal transducer film (70-100 nm, typically aluminum) deposited on the sample of interest is heated by a pump optical pulse, and the resulting change in optical reflectance is measured by a time-delayed probe pulse. Free parameters in an analytical thermal model — typically the sample's thermal conductivity and the transducer/sample interface conductance — are adjusted to fit the measured signal, with transducer film thickness independently determined by picosecond acoustics and transducer thermal conductivity estimated from electrical conductivity via the Wiedemann-Franz law. The diffusion-based analytical model is valid for pump-probe delay times greater than approximately 100 ps in crystalline substrates; shorter-time behavior requires treatment of quasi-ballistic phonon transport.

6. RISK ASSESSMENT AND MITIGATION

Risk: Insufficient sensitivity to interface conductance variations for certain material pairs. If the sample's bulk thermal conductivity dominates the measured thermal response, sensitivity to the interface conductance parameter may be too low to extract a reliable value. Mitigation: modulation frequency and transducer thickness can be adjusted (higher modulation frequency and/or thinner transducer increase relative sensitivity to the interface) to recover adequate sensitivity for a given material system.

Risk: Diffusion-model invalidity at short delay times. Because the diffusion picture is not expected to hold for delay times shorter than approximately 100 ps in crystalline substrates, any measurement or model fitting that depends on very short-time data risks misinterpretation. Mitigation: quantitative analysis is restricted to delay times beyond this validated threshold, and departures from diffusion-model predictions at shorter times are treated as evidence of quasi-ballistic transport rather than measurement error.

Risk: Sample-to-sample variability in interface preparation. Bulk disorder and imperfect physical contact between the transducer and sample can affect measured interface conductance independently of the intrinsic material chemistry being studied. Mitigation: multiple samples are prepared and measured under nominally identical conditions to characterize this variability directly, and results are interpreted relative to that measured spread rather than treated as exact single-point values.

7. DATA MANAGEMENT APPROACH

Raw TDTR data consists of lock-in amplifier output (in-phase and out-of-phase components) as a function of pump-probe delay time, modulation frequency, and, where relevant, spot size — together with sample characterization data such as picosecond acoustic film-thickness traces and electrical conductivity measurements. Processed data consists of extracted thermal conductivity and interface conductance values together with their associated sensitivity/uncertainty estimates.
