Team Qualifications, Management Approach, and Execution Plan: Dynamic Mechanical Behavior of Additively Manufactured Metals

1. AWARD TYPE AND PROPOSAL STRUCTURE

This proposal is submitted against the ARL Broad Agency Announcement for Foundational Research as an Army Research Office Single Investigator award. Per the BAA, ARO's standard funding level for a Single Investigator award supports a budget for three years commensurate with a single investigator plus one or two graduate students or a postdoctoral researcher, inclusive of facilities and administrative costs. The staffing and schedule described below are scoped to that structure rather than to a larger team effort.

The proposal is submitted by the institution employing the Principal Investigator, and any resulting award is made to the institution rather than to the investigator.

2. PRINCIPAL INVESTIGATOR QUALIFICATIONS

The Principal Investigator designed and executed the preliminary study described in the technical background, which established the core experimental capability this proposal builds on: split-Hopkinson (Kolsky) pressure bar testing with strain rate determined from measured bar signals, X-ray computed tomography defect characterization at 8 micrometer voxel resolution, high-speed digital image correlation during dynamic loading, and pre-test to post-test XCT registration enabling spatial association of localization sites with pre-existing defect features.

That preliminary work produced the finding that motivates this proposal — the roughly threefold asymmetry between quasi-static and dynamic sensitivity to a lack-of-fusion defect population in L-PBF 316L — and demonstrated that the effect is measurable well above experimental scatter with the available instrumentation. The PI additionally established that the experimental approach transfers without modification to L-PBF Ti-6Al-4V, through a three-specimen screening study.

4. MANAGEMENT APPROACH

Technical direction and internal coordination. Weekly working meetings between the PI and the graduate researcher will review measurement results, data reduction, and next-step test planning. Because the experimental matrix is sequential — defect characterization under Objective 1 sets the replicate count and specimen sizing for Objective 2, which in turn supplies the data for Objective 4 — decisions at each stage gate materially affect downstream work, and those decisions will be documented rather than made informally.

Government interaction. The BAA strongly encourages a preliminary inquiry to the cognizant ARO Technical Point of Contact before submission, and encourages whitepaper submission for TPOC review prior to a full proposal. That sequence will be followed: preliminary inquiry to establish alignment with a published ARL BAA research topic and funding availability, then a whitepaper of no more than five pages as a single PDF identifying the research topic name, topic identification number, and TPOC, then the full proposal. Following award, technical interaction with the TPOC will follow whatever cadence is established in the award document.

Data quality control. Strain rate will be determined from measured transmitted and reflected bar signals for every dynamic test rather than nominally assigned. A repeat of the nominally optimal process condition will be included in every build as a control, so that build-to-build variability is measured rather than assumed absent. Interstitial oxygen content will be measured on witness specimens from each Ti-6Al-4V build. Held-out process conditions for Objective 4 validation will be designated before calibration begins, not selected afterward.

Reporting. The award document will specify reporting requirements, which per the BAA include at minimum monthly financial status reports, along with reports and briefing material as appropriate to document progress against program metrics, and a final report summarizing the project and tasks at the conclusion of the period of performance.

5. SCHEDULE, MILESTONES, AND DECISION POINTS

Year 1, first half. Build design and specimen production across the energy density range for L-PBF 316L. Multi-resolution XCT characterization to establish defect population statistics including morphology, size distribution, aspect ratio, and spatial clustering. EBSD and metallographic characterization of grain structure, cellular substructure, and texture for each condition.

Milestone at end of Year 1 first half, and first decision point: defect spacing statistics compared against planned specimen dimensions to confirm the representative volume element condition. If defect spacing is comparable to specimen dimensions, replicate counts are increased accordingly and results will be reported explicitly as single-defect-population realizations rather than as bulk properties. This determination governs the entire Objective 2 test matrix and is therefore made before that matrix is executed.

Year 1, second half. Quasi-static and intermediate-rate mechanical testing of 316L across all conditions. Initiation of dynamic SHPB testing. Establishment of the defect state descriptor from Objective 1 characterization data.

Milestone at end of Year 1: candidate defect state descriptor defined and shown to distinguish lack-of-fusion from keyhole populations independent of total porosity.

Year 2, first half. Completion of the 316L dynamic test matrix across the full strain-rate range from 10^-3 to 10^4 per second. High-speed DIC on all dynamic tests, with pre-test and post-test XCT registration on recovered specimens.

Milestone: the quasi-static versus dynamic sensitivity asymmetry established or refuted with statistical confidence across the rate range for 316L. This is the central Objective 2 result.

Second decision point at Year 2 midpoint: whether the defect-to-localization association observed preliminarily is supported prospectively. Localization sites are predicted from pre-test defect maps and scored against observation. If prospective prediction fails, Objective 3 is redirected from initiation-site prediction toward characterizing what else governs localization, and this redirection is reported as a substantive negative result rather than absorbed silently.

Year 2, second half. Ti-6Al-4V specimen production and characterization across the energy density range. Beginning of Ti-6Al-4V mechanical testing. Quantitative fractography on recovered 316L specimens including shear band width measurement where bands are present.

Year 3, first half. Completion of Ti-6Al-4V testing across strain rate. Constitutive model formulation with the defect descriptor as internal state variable, calibrated on the designated calibration subset.

Year 3, second half. Blind validation of the constitutive model against the held-out process conditions for both alloys, with held-out prediction error reported rather than calibration-set fit quality. Manuscript preparation and submission. Final report.

Milestone at end of Year 3: held-out prediction error quantified and reported, constituting the Objective 4 result and the basis for the TRL 2 to TRL 3 advancement claim.

6. RISK MANAGEMENT

Risk: representative volume element condition not satisfiable at standard specimen size. Likelihood moderate; impact moderate. The defect populations of interest may have spacing comparable to viable SHPB specimen dimensions, in which case individual specimens sample the population sparsely and scatter grows. Mitigation: this is tested at the first decision point before the main matrix is committed, with replicate count set from measured spacing statistics. Contingency: results reported as single-realization measurements with explicit statistical characterization of specimen-to-specimen variability, which remains a valid and publishable result, rather than as bulk property values.

Risk: microstructural covariation cannot be separated from defect-population effects. Likelihood moderate; impact high, since it would undermine the central causal claim. Mitigation: EBSD and metallographic descriptors are entered jointly with the defect descriptor so the contributions are estimated together rather than one absorbing the other's effect. Contingency: if the two cannot be statistically separated within the accessible process window, that limitation is reported as a bound on the inference. This would weaken Objective 4 but would still constitute a useful and honest result about the limits of process-parameter-based prediction.

Risk: the defect descriptor fails to predict held-out process conditions. Likelihood moderate; impact high for the stated Objective 4 success criterion. This is the principal scientific risk of the effort and the reason it is proposed as basic research. Mitigation: descriptor candidates are developed and compared during Year 1 rather than a single form being committed to early. Contingency: a negative held-out validation result, clearly reported alongside diagnosis of which descriptor features failed, is a legitimate scientific outcome that redirects follow-on work rather than a project failure — and is considerably more useful to the field than an unreported calibration-only fit.

Risk: build quality or powder lot variability confounds the process-parameter study. Likelihood low to moderate; impact moderate. Mitigation: powder lot, layer thickness, and scan strategy held fixed within each build; nominally optimal condition repeated as a control in every build; oxygen content measured on Ti-6Al-4V witness specimens. Contingency: any build failing control comparison is treated as a separate condition rather than pooled with others.

Risk: graduate researcher recruitment or continuity. Likelihood low to moderate; impact moderate given the single-student staffing structure, in which departure would materially affect schedule.
